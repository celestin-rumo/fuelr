# v3.3.0 — 2026-09-06

Five things, and none of them is a feature somebody asked for while using the
app. This is the release that fixes what was quietly missing: the backups that
did not exist, the site that was in the wrong language, the contrast nobody had
measured, the operator with no way in, and the photograph you had to take
somewhere else.

## The data is backed up, and the backup is proved

The `recipe_media` volume was in no backup, and neither was Postgres. It was
the only item on the backlog where waiting cost something that does not come
back.

Both are taken now — a dump without the photographs restores a library of grey
rectangles — and the database is dumped **first**, on purpose: a photo uploaded
between the two lands in the archive with no row pointing at it, which costs a
few kilobytes, where the other order gives a row pointing at a file that is not
in the backup, which is a missing photograph.

**What makes it more than a dump: every backup is restored into a throwaway
Postgres before it is kept**, and the accounts, recipes and reference foods are
counted. An empty database restores perfectly, so "it restored" is not the
claim worth making. A backup nobody has restored is a hypothesis, and the day
it is needed is the worst possible day to find out.

Putting one back defaults to a **drill** — a throwaway on port 15432 that says
what came back, which is the command to run on an ordinary Tuesday, because a
restore procedure nobody has run is a document rather than a procedure. The
real one refuses to move until the backup's own name is typed back, and dumps
what it is about to replace first.

`BACKUPS.md` is the whole procedure. One thing it says loudly: until an
off-host copy is configured, these sit on the same disk as the data. That
covers a mistake; it covers nothing that happens to the machine.

## The public site is in the visitor's language

`site.json` was French in all three locales — 201 of its 250 strings. An
English or German visitor landed on the front page and read French.

Both catalogues are filled now: the hero, the features, the pricing plans and
their feature lists, the FAQ, the about page, the contact form and its errors,
the footer, and the whole privacy page. The German is **Swiss** German: no
eszett, and the guillemets Switzerland uses rather than the German pair.

And the stats band said **`0 €`** on a product priced in francs, in every
locale including the French one.

The test that guarded this asserted the localised *slug*. A translated URL over
French words is precisely the failure that hid it, so it asserts the rendered
copy now.

## Usable without a mouse, and measured

The intentions were already right — a focus ring on every control, icons hidden
from screen readers with the name on the control, a dialog that traps focus.
What was missing is the thing that keeps any of it true: nothing measured it.

**The design system claimed its contrast ratios in a table nobody had
computed, and one of them was wrong.** `--gray` carries every label, hint and
meta line — text, at 11 and 12 pixels — and it sat at 3.3:1 against the 4.5:1
WCAG asks. It takes a lighter step in dark now, and that single token cleared
thirteen failing screens.

Two things hold it: a test that reads `globals.css` and computes every text
token against every surface, in a second and with no browser; and a suite that
runs axe over sixteen screens and then does what axe cannot — Tab reaching a
row, the focused control drawing an outline, Escape closing a menu and handing
focus back, a dialog opening with focus inside it.

**The first Tab is now a skip link.** Without one, reaching the content costs a
dozen presses through the same header, on every page, forever.

## An operator's panel

`/admin`, with accounts, subscriptions, usage and AI costs. One place where the
role is checked, and it answers **404, not 403**: a panel that exists only for
the operator has no reason to confirm to anybody else that it exists. The costs
page becomes a section of it, and its old address redirects.

**Deleting an account would have deleted somebody else's week.** The schema set
a trap and it was a chain — a household cascades from its owner, and the
planned meals cascade from the household — so erasing one person who asked to
be erased would have silently taken away every meal every *member* had put on
that shared week. The household passes to its longest-standing member instead,
and the dialog says so, from what the server reports rather than from a
sentence written in the screen.

A tier granted by hand goes through the same path a payment will, so it is a
plan like any other; who granted it and why is written down. And the figures
say **committed**, never *revenue*: no provider is wired, every plan was
granted, and a theoretical number shown as received is how a dashboard starts
lying.

## Photograph a page, and keep only the recipe

The photo import could already read a page of a cookbook. What was missing was
the way in: you had to *already have* the image, so the natural gesture — the
book is open in front of me, now — went through four screens, three of them not
ours.

It hands off to the **system** camera rather than opening a preview in the
page. The native app brings autofocus, exposure and the whole sensor, which on
small print is the difference between a reading that works and one that fails —
and a failed reading is billed like a successful one.

Pictures accumulate, because a recipe spans two pages. Each is a thumbnail
before anything is sent and each can be removed. And each can be **cropped**:
the frame starts on the whole image, so doing nothing sends everything, and it
is applied once, at the moment of sending. Cropping is not only framing — what
goes to a model is billed by its size, so cutting away the facing page removes
tokens as well as noise.

## Verification

Backend 296, frontend unit 258, e2e 234 — green on the merge commit.

CI's backend now gets a key that cannot be billed, which opens the assisted
sources so the browser-side half of the import — taking a photograph, framing
it, cropping it — is actually exercised there. It never was before.

Four traps are written into `CLAUDE.md` rather than left to be rediscovered: a
handle hanging outside an `overflow-hidden` container stops being hit-testable
and the drag fails silently; a hand-written base64 "PNG" is not a PNG; a Maven
container run without `--user` leaves root-owned files in `backend/target/`;
and a dependency installed in the dev container is invisible to the end-to-end
run, which reads the host's `node_modules`.

## What is left

**Nothing about payment works yet**, and it is still the largest ticket: no
provider is chosen, so the screen says the plan is not open yet. Choosing one
means writing one class behind `PaymentProvider`, and the admin panel's billing
section waits on the same thing.

**The backups now exist but do not leave the machine.** Configuring
`FUELR_BACKUP_POST_HOOK` is a one-line change and the last half of that story.

Everything paid for remains open to everybody: `app.subscription.enforce` is
off, and reading an image or a page with a model is capped at 10 CHF a month
per account and 50 across all of them.
