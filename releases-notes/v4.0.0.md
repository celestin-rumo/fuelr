# v4.0.0 — 2026-09-07

One epic: *Mon compte*. What is yours stays under your hand — your profile,
your data, and the door out.

Until now the account had no page. The profile express was written once at
registration and never shown again; the name and the address had no screen at
all; the only way to change a password was to forget it; and leaving meant
writing to somebody. Six stories, and every one of them is about the same
thing: nothing about you should be something you cannot see, correct, take
with you, or end.

## A page for what Fuelr knows about you

`/app/compte`, from your name in the header. Four forms on it, each its own
transaction, because one big form with one save button would have to explain
four different failures with one message.

**Your name** saves on its own. **Your language** follows the account rather
than the browser, and the page follows the language — saving it lands you on
the same page in the other locale, which is the only honest confirmation
there is.

**Your address is your login**, so it is not an edit. Changing it needs your
password, sends a link to the *new* address and a notice to the *old* one,
and nothing moves until the link is clicked; the new address arrives verified
because the click was the proof. Whether the address is already taken is
never confessed — the reply is the same either way, and what differs is in
the mail, exactly as at registration.

**A password change that leaves your other devices signed in has changed
nothing** for whoever holds the previous one. So they are signed out, this
one stays, and a mail says so with the way to a reset for the case where it
was not you. Neither password field is ever pre-filled.

**The six figures** the journal's target is computed from — age, height,
weight, activity, goal — are shown, corrected, and previewed before they are
written. A target is shown, never sprung, and the page says what it is: a
formula, not a prescription.

## Weigh-ins, drawn and never judged

The profile held one weight, written the day you signed up. Six months later
the journal's target was computed on a weight nobody had.

There is a history now: one figure a day, weighing twice replaces. The
profile keeps its own figure on purpose — it is what the target is computed
from, and it moves only when you say so. When your latest weigh-in has
drifted a kilo from it, the panel *offers* the recalculation with what it
changes; only a press writes it.

Nothing is said about the curve. One series, one accent, a day you did not
weigh left as a gap rather than drawn as zero, no streak and no verdict — the
same rule as the journal's findings. A curve of weight is the field where an
application most easily starts to judge, and this one draws a figure and its
date and stops. Free, because it is your own number.

## What I do not eat, said once

Since the planner started inventing its dishes, an allergy repeated at every
ask is an allergy forgotten once — and the once it is forgotten, the dish is
on the plan.

A diet and up to fourteen allergens — the European declaration's list, both
closed for the reason the seasons are: "without peanuts" has to be
computable. They reach every screen that proposes a dish so the answer is
likely to comply, and then **the code reads the ingredient lines that come
back and drops a dish that names one, whatever the model was told**. The
library can be narrowed the same way. An allergy filtered by the model alone
is not filtered; it is the one place in this application where being wrong
makes somebody ill, and the test proves it with a stand-in model that ignores
the instruction on purpose.

A free line — "je n'aime pas la coriandre" — reaches a model quoted, as
something you said, and nothing acts on it. The card says which box protects
you, because "no peanuts" typed there would not.

## My devices

Where the account is signed in, as "Chrome · Linux" and a last-seen time — the
browser family and the platform, in words, never an address and never the raw
string, because the point is recognising your own phone, not fingerprinting
it. Close one, or close all the others. This device is marked and closes only
through sign-out, so the button that removes a session can never remove the
one you are pressing it from. A session closed elsewhere lands you on a login
page that says so.

## Everything I have, in one archive

Recipes and their photos, the meals *you* put on the plan, the shopping lists
of the household you own, the journal with its figures as they were when you
wrote them, your profile, your weigh-ins, your preferences. Built in the
background, because a library with photos is not built inside a click, and
sent as a link that works once, for a day, after which the archive is erased
whether you took it or not. What is not inside says so in the README: meals
other members put on a shared plan, lists of a household you only belong to,
and the AI cost rows, which are the operator's.

## Leaving

Deleting the account no longer means writing to anybody. It asks for your
password — an open tab must not be enough — and the dialog says what it will
do from what the server reports: "your 43 recipes", "your household passes to
Anna". Nothing that belongs to somebody else is deleted; the same code the
operator's panel uses does the work, because a second way to delete an
account is the one that leaves photos behind. The export is offered once on
the way, and never blocks. The privacy page stops saying "by hand".

## Recommending, and one email

A link to share and a message ready to send. **Not a referral programme**: no
plan is paid for, so there is nothing to offer, and promising a free month
for a subscription that does not exist would be the pricing page in reverse.
The account sees how many came by the link, never who. One thirty-day cookie
remembers the code until you register; no pixel, no third party, and the
privacy page names it.

And a weekly reminder — the only email Fuelr sends without having to, and
**off until you ask for it**. Sent on the day and at the hour you chose, its
content decided at the moment of sending: an empty coming week, or a list
that is ready; when there is nothing worth saying, nothing is sent. Every one
carries a one-click stop. The verification and password mails cannot be
turned off, because those are not preferences.

## Why 4.0

Nothing here breaks a client. The major is for what the release changes about
the relationship: the account is now a thing you own end to end — read it,
correct it, take it, end it — and that is a different promise from the one
v3 made.

## What is left

**No payment provider is chosen**, so subscribing, cancelling and the admin
panel's billing section wait on one decision. **The backups still do not
leave the machine.** **Photos are the missing half of offline.** And the
pricing page names one of the three AI capabilities as paid where there are
three.
