# v3.2.0 — 2026-09-04

The library stops being a wall of buttons above a list you scroll, and becomes
the place a recipe is actually put to use.

## Cook it, or plan it, without opening it

Two things somebody opens the library to do were both several screens away
from the list where the choice is made. They are now on the row.

**Planning asks two questions and pre-answers both** — today, and dinner.
Opening the planner, finding the week, finding the day and dragging a card is
four decisions for something already decided when the recipe was picked. It
plans into the week today falls in, and the banner afterwards links to the
planner, which is where a different week is chosen. Nothing is copied: a
planned meal points at the recipe, and the servings default to the household
size, which is why they are not asked for here.

**Cooking is a link**, so it opens in a new tab like any other.

Making room for those two meant a menu. The frequent actions stay in the rail;
edit, duplicate, reorder and delete moved one press away. Seven controls in a
rail is 308px of buttons on a 360px screen, and the two that matter would have
been the ones to wrap. The rule that made the rail worth fixing in the first
place is unchanged: the trigger sits in the same place on every row, and what
does not apply is disabled inside the menu rather than missing from it.

## Three buttons become one

New, import and export was 428px of controls on a screen that has 320 — on a
phone they stacked into a wall three deep above the recipes. They also read as
three equals, which they are not: creating is what somebody came to do,
importing is occasional, exporting is a twice-a-year errand.

One primary action now, and the other two behind the same press.

## Six recipes a page

A library of two hundred is not a decision anybody makes by scrolling. The
filters narrow it; the pages are how what is left gets read.

No numbered pages: a row of numbers is a row of 44px targets a 360px screen
cannot hold. The position is a sentence rather than "3 / 12", and it is
announced when it changes — pressing "next" moves rows somebody cannot see.

## Two placements that were wrong

**"Une idée ?" moves out of the header**, where it sat beside the account
controls. That is where chrome lives, and it is not chrome: it is what to do
when the list you have just read did not answer the question. So it sits under
that list.

**The suggestions use the library's own rows.** Two ways of drawing "a dish,
its time, what it would cost to make" is one too many. It also settles the
illustration question honestly: no row anywhere in this application carries a
photograph, so an idea is no longer made to look like it is missing one.

## The operator can be named

`/total-costs` was reachable only by typing a URL nobody had written down. It
has a link in the app header now, visible to an admin and to nobody else —
the same rule the page and the endpoint already followed.

Which raised the second half: *which* account is the admin? `ADMIN_EMAIL` was
read once, at the very first boot, and ignored ever after — so pointing it at
the address somebody actually uses did nothing, and "make my account the
operator" had exactly one answer: a hand-run `UPDATE` on the production
database. Nobody can replay that, nobody reviews it, and a restore forgets it.

It now **promotes the account holding that address**, at every boot. Same
account, same password, same recipes. Three refusals, and each is the
interesting half:

- **It never demotes.** Losing the role must not be a side effect of a
  variable changing — the last admin vanishing on a redeploy is how an
  installation locks its owner out.
- **It never touches a password.** An account that already exists keeps the
  one its owner set, so this cannot be used to take an account over.
  `ADMIN_PASSWORD` is only ever used for an account being created here.
- **An address nobody holds creates an account only when there is no admin at
  all**, so a typo cannot quietly add a second operator with a password from
  an environment file.

## Verification

Backend 284, frontend unit 236, e2e 199 — green on the merge commit. The new
end-to-end tests cover what the change is actually for: cooking and planning
straight from a row, paging past the sixth recipe, and an operator having a
way in while nobody else sees one. The four new backend tests are all about
what the promotion refuses to do.

Two things were found on the way and written into `CLAUDE.md`. A rail's
controls sit at `z-10`, and `position: relative` with a z-index creates a
stacking context — so a menu panel at `z-40` *inside* a `z-10` wrapper still
lost to the next row's controls and they showed through it. And a Maven
container launched without `--user` leaves root-owned files in
`backend/target/`, where the failure looks nothing like its cause.

## What is left

**Nothing about payment works yet**, and it is still the largest ticket: no
provider is chosen, so `canOrder` is false and the screen says the plan is not
open yet. Choosing one means writing one class behind `PaymentProvider`.

**The backup gap is still the one where waiting costs data.** The
`recipe_media` volume is in no backup, and neither is Postgres.

**The public site is still not translated** — `site.json` is French in all
three locales, and several footer links resolve to a generic page.

The **admin panel** epic is untouched. Everything paid for remains open to
everybody: `app.subscription.enforce` is off, and reading an image or a page
with a model is capped at 10 CHF a month per account and 50 across all of them.
